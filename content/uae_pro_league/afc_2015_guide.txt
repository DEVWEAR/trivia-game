W
estern Sydney Wanderers’ 
historic victory over Al Hilal 
from Saudi Arabia in the final 
of the 2014 AFC Champions 
League in November was a true 
milestone for Australian football. 
Six teams from Australia had 
stepped up to the continental 
challenge without success since A-League sides joined 
the AFC Champions League in 2007 before the team from 
Parramatta, in their maiden appearance in the competition, 
went all the way to lift the prestigious trophy.
Tony Popovic’s Western Sydney edged out Al Hilal 1-0 on 
aggregate, a victory which went one better than predecessors 
Adelaide United who fell short at the final hurdle after losing 
to Japan’s Gamba Osaka in 2008.
Tomi Juric’s 64th minute strike in October’s first leg at 
Parramatta Stadium had given Western Sydney a crucial 1-0 
lead to take to the return meeting in Riyadh.
And it proved enough as two-time Asian Club Championship 
winners Al Hilal failed to convert a dominant performance 
in front of nearly 64,000 at the King Fahd International 
Stadium into a breakthrough as Western Sydney held on to 
become the first A-League side to win the AFC Champions 
League title.
“The game was everything you expect from a final. It 
was difficult. They are a very good team, they had fantastic 
support, but we are an excellent team and we showed over 
two legs how good we are,” said Western Sydney coach 
Popovic following the second leg. 
“It is still a little surreal for me as a coach. I am sure it will 
hit home in the next few days when we reflect. For me, I am 
just proud for these players and our club. The first time in 
the competition, to win it, I think in the future we will really 
understand how special this run has been and how it was to 
win this title.”
Western Sydney goalkeeper Ante Covic was named the AFC 
Champions League MVP after his ninth clean sheet on the 
continent in the second leg of the final, which was arguably 
the 39-year-old’s best as the goalkeeper made a number of 
vital saves including a superb one-handed block to deny Al 
Hilal substitute Yasser Al Qahtani inside the final five minutes. 
“It is unbelievable,” said Covic. “To come away in the short 
history of this club and win a title like this is something you 
carry with you for the rest of your life.”
Victory in the AFC Champions League also saw Western 
Sydney named the AFC’s Club Team of the Year, while Popovic 
won the AFC Coach of the Year accolade.

History
Western Sydney’s triumph marked the end of an eight year wait for 
an Australian club to lift Asia’s premier prize after neighbours Sydney FC 
and Adelaide were the first two sides to participate in the AFC Champions 
League in 2007.
With only one team from each group qualifying from the knockout stage 
of that tournament, both sides were eliminated in the group stage, although 
Sydney were particularly unlucky to miss out on a place in the quarter-
finals after being edged out by a point by group winners, and eventual 
champions, Urawa Red Diamonds from Japan.
Before the A-League sides joined the scene, teams from West Asia had 
dominated the revamped continental championship after winning three of 
the first four editions of the new AFC Champions League; a merger of 
the Asian Club Championship, the Asian Cup Winners’ Cup and the Asian 
Super Cup into a consolidated competition that was inaugurated in 2002. 
Inaugural winners Al Ain of the United Arab Emirates and Saudi Arabia’s 
Al Ittihad, in both 2004 and 2005, took the first three titles before Korea 
from Tokyo as Seongnam Ilhwa Chunma downed Zobahan from Iran while 
Melbourne, making their second appearance, again failed to progress to the 
knockout stages. In their third continental campaign, Adelaide were edged 
out by Jeonbuk following a thrilling Round of 16 tie that could only be 
decided after extra-time. 
The 2011 campaign was a more forgettable one for teams from Australia 
as for the first time since their maiden tournament neither A-League 
representative made the knockout stage with both Melbourne and Sydney’s 
Asian adventure ending in the group stage. Instead it was a return to form 
for the west as Qatar’s Al Sadd edged out Jeonbuk on penalties in the final. 
The following year’s competition, though, brought a return to form with 
Adelaide’s play-off victory over Persipura Jayapura meaning three teams 
from Australia participated in the AFC Champions League for the first time. 
And while debutants Brisbane Roar as well as Central Coast were unable to 
reach the Round of 16, Adelaide topped their group and then edged out 
Nagoya Grampus of Japan 1-0 to reach the quarter-finals before taking part 
Republic’s Jeonbuk Hyundai Motors broke the stranglehold, becoming the 
first winner from the east in 2006. 
Urawa swiftly followed a year later when the J.League side claimed the 
title in front of a bumper crowd at Saitama Stadium after beating Iran’s 
Sepahan. Although the Australian clubs were unable to make a significant 
impact in their maiden campaign, their second year was far more successful 
as Adelaide far outstripped countrymen Melbourne Victory by going all the 
way to the 2008 final, although they fell at the last hurdle after losing 5-0 
on aggregate to Gamba.
In 2009, two A-League sides made their debuts in the AFC Champions 
League as Central Coast Mariners and Newcastle Jets took part in their 
maiden continental campaigns, and although Central Coast bowed out in 
the group stage, Newcastle reached the Round of 16 only to be outmatched 
by eventual winners Pohang Steelers from Korea Republic who defeated Al 
Ittihad in the final in Tokyo.                                                              
And 12 months later it was another Korean team emerging triumphant 
in an epic tie with Uzbekistan’s Bunyodkor, that the Central Asians would 
eventually win 5-4 on aggregate. 
Bunyodkor, though, would be on the end of a comprehensive 5-1 defeat 
to Ulsan Hyundai in the semi-final and the K-League side replicated that 
dominance in the final after beating Saudi Arabia’s Al Ahli 3-0 in the 
tournament showpiece. It was a case of feast to famine in 2013 for the 
A-League as just one club from Australia made it to the group stage of 
the AFC Champions League after Brisbane were knocked out of the play-
offs by Buriram United of Thailand. Sole representative Central Coast did, 
however, advance to the Round of 16 for their first appearance in the 
knockout stages. But Central Coast were confronted by Marcello Lippi’s 
Guangzhou Evergrande and the FIFA World Cup winning-coach would guide 
his team past the A-League side on the way to China’s first continental 
title in 23 years after defeating Korea’s FC Seoul in the final on away 
goals. Western Sydney joined Melbourne and Central Coast in the 2014 
competition, with Popovic’s side making their debut in the tournament

Overview
With previous pedigree in continental competition, Al Nassr will make just their second 
appearance in the AFC Champions League in 2015 after being crowned Saudi League 
champions for the seventh time. In 2014 having finished the season two points ahead 
of cross-city rivals Al Hilal. In their first AFC Champions League campaign in 2011, 
Al Nassr progressed beyond the group stage behind eventual champions Al Sadd, but 
were eliminated by Iran’s Zobahan after suffering a 4-1 defeat in their Round of 16 
tie. Runner-up in the Asian Club Championship in 1995, Al Nassr also claimed both the 
Asian Cup Winners’ Cup and Asian Super Cup in 1998. 
Key Players
Mohammed Al Sahlawi scored 17 goals at an average of just over a goal a game as Al 
Nassr won the Saudi League last year. And although Saudi Arabia were eliminated in the 
group stage of the AFC Asian Cup Australia 2015, Al Sahlawi netted three times after 
finding the net twice against DPR Korea and also against Uzbekistan. An ever-present 
in the backline for Al Nassr, Saudi Arabia centre-back Omar Hawsawi was a fixture 
in the side that took part in the 2011 AFC Champions League and started all three 
games for his national team at the AFC Asian Cup Australia 2015. Poland international 
Adrian Mierzejewski was signed ahead of the 2014 season and the former Trabzonspor 
midfielder has already become an important member of the Al Nassr side as well as a 
significant goal threat.  
Coach: Jorge Da Silva
Former Uruguay international forward Jorge Da Silva joined Al Nassr for the first time 
in 2009 and led the side to a third place finish in the league and the quarter-finals 
of the Crown Prince Cup. And after a spell in the United Arab Emirates, the 53-year-
old returned to Al Nassr in November 2014 for a second stint at the club. Previously, 
the former Spanish league top scorer led both Defensor Sporting and Penarol to win 
domestic titles in Uruguay.

Overview
Originally founded in 1938 as Al Shorta, Lekhwiya secured their fourth consecutive 
appearance in the AFC Champions League when they were crowned Qatar Stars League 
champions for a third time in four seasons in 2014. Lekhwiya won back-to-back 
domestic titles in 2011 and 2012 and also finished runners-up in 2013. A group stage 
elimination in 2014 after a successful route through the play-offs saw the Qatari side 
unable to build on their best-ever performance in the AFC Champions League during 
the previous year when Lekhwiya reached the knockout stage for the first time. They 
beat Saudi Arabia’s Al Hilal in the Round of 16 only to lose to eventual champions 
Guangzhou Evergrande in the quarter-finals.  
Key Players
Spaniard Chico Flores reunited with former Swansea City manager Michael Laudrup in 
August 2014 and the central defender, who played for Mallorca, Genoa and Almeria, 
brings solidity and experience to the backline. Qatar forward Ismail Mohamad has been 
a part of his country’s most successful period in recent times after winning the 2014 
WAFF Championship and 2014 Gulf Cup titles. Versatile midfielder Nam Tae-hee played 
an important role in Korea Republic’s runner-up finish at the AFC Asian Cup Australia 
2015 and made four appearances including the final while also scoring the only goal in 
the win over Kuwait in the group stage. Nam, who also helped Korea win a bronze medal 
at the 2012 Olympic Games, was Lekhwiya’s joint top scorer during the 2013/2014 
season with 12 goals.   
Coach: Michael Laudrup 
One of the icons of Danish football, Michael Laudrup made a name for himself as a 
player after winning league titles with Ajax, Barcelona, Real Madrid and Juventus. After 
hanging his boots up Laudrup had huge success as a coach with Brondby where he won 
the Danish Superliga and the Danish Cup and he was named the Danish Manager of the 
Year on two occasions. He had several coaching stints in Russia, Spain and Wales with 
Swansea City before joining Lekhwiya in June 2014.

Overview
Winners of the Uzbek League in five of the previous six seasons, Bunyodkor finished 
a disappointing fourth in 2014 but secured a spot in the group stage of the AFC 
Champions League for the eighth season in a row after they defeated Al Jazira of 
the United Arab Emirates in the play-offs. While the Tashkent club have yet to reach 
an AFC Champions League final, Bunyodkor have a solid record in the continental 
tournament after making it to the semi-finals in 2008 and 2012 and qualifying for 
the knockout stage in all seven of their previous appearances. Formed as Kuruvchi in 
2005, Bunyodkor rose quickly through the domestic leagues and qualified for the AFC 
Champions League just three seasons later.   
Key Players
Sardor Rashidov was the match-winner against Al Jazira in the play-off for the AFC 
Champions League following a successful AFC Asian Cup Australia 2015 as the striker 
helped Uzbekistan reach the quarter-finals. Rashidov featured in all four games in 
Australia having scored 15 goals for Bunyodkor in all competitions in 2014. Vokhid 
Shodiev is now in his second season with Bunyodkor after joining from Buxoro ahead 
of the 2014 season. Also a member of the national team, Shodiev was also on target 
during Bunyodkor’s 2-1 victory over Al Jazira in the play-offs. Well-travelled Japanese 
midfielder Minori Sato has already ticked off spells with clubs in the United States, 
Mexico, Latvia and Belarus by the age of 23 and the tricky playmaker is an important 
creative cog in the Bunyodkor midfield having joined the club in March 2014.   
Coach: Sergey Lushchan  
Former Uzbekistan international and Bunyodkor player Sergey Lushchan joined the 
club’s coaching structure after winding up his playing career in 2008. After roles with 
the youth academy, youth team and reserve side, Lushchan was appointed coach in June 
2014 following a month’s spell with the Uzbekistan U-19 team. As a player, Lushchan 
won the Uzbek League and Cup double in 2008, although as a manager he is still 
seeking his first silverware after Bunyodkor finished fourth.
Overview
One of Iran’s most popular sides, Persepolis return to the AFC Champions League for 
the first time since 2012 after finishing runners-up in the Iran Pro league in 2014, 
just two points behind champions Foolad Khouzestan. Winners of a record nine Iranian 
championships, Persepolis won the inaugural Asian Cup Winners’ Cup in 1991 and have 
progressed to the knockout stage of the AFC Champions League on two occasions in 
2009 and 2012. With group stage exits also in 2003 and 2011, last time out in the AFC 
Champions League, Persepolis progressed from the group stage only to lose to Saudi 
Arabia’s Al Ittihad in 2012.  
Key Players
Named the 2014 Iran Football Federation’s Young Player of the Year, Mohammad 
Abbaszadeh is continuing to show the form that his debut season in Iran’s top flight 
promised. Having joined Persepolis in 2013, after he was top scorer in the second tier 
with Nassaji Mazandaran, striker Abbaszadeh has already established himself as a key 
member of the squad and key source of goals.  Experienced club captain Mohammad 
Nouri is the veteran presence amongst a youthful side after the midfielder signed from 
Saba Battery in 2010. Veteran defender Mohsen Bengar  helped Sepahan reach the 2007 
AFC Champions League final before moving to Tehran and Persepolis in 2012. During 
his time with Sepahan, former Iran international Bengar won the Iranian league title 
once and the Hazfi Cup in two occasions.  
Coach: Hamid Derakhshan
Former Iran captain Hamid Derakhshan retuned to former club Persepolis as coach 
in September 2014. Derakhshan spent 12 years as a player with Persepolis before 
taking over as coach following his retirement in 1994. During his time as a player with 
Persepolis, Derakhshan won the Asian Cup Winners’ Cup in 1991. He also coached Iran’s 
U-17 national team and several clubs including Shahin Bushehr who he led to the 2012 
Hazfi Cup final.

Overview
Saudi Araba’s Al Hilal fell just short of landing a record-equalling third continental 
crown at the end of 2014 after losing the AFC Champions League final 1-0 on 
aggregate to Australia’s Western Sydney Wanderers. Al Hilal previously won the Asian 
Club Championship in 1991 and 2000, while also winning the Asian Cup Winners’ Cup 
and Asian Super Cup on two occasions. Prior to 2014, Al Hilal’s best performance in the 
AFC Champions League arrived in 2010 as they reached the semi-finals before losing to 
Iran’s Zobahan. Al Hilal have also claimed a record 13 Saudi League titles, but finished 
just two points behind champions Al Nassr in 2014.  
Key Players
A back-to-back AFC Champions League winner in 2004 and 2005 with Al Ittihad, 
veteran midfielder Saud Kariri helped Al Hilal to the final in last year’s competition.

country, has appeared in four AFC Asian Cups as well as the 2006 FIFA World Cup. 
Kariri’s leadership on the field will be especially important for Al Hilal in the absence of 
the suspended striker and 2014 AFC Player of the Year Nassir Al Shamrani. Centre-back 
Kwak Tae-hwi captained Ulsan Hyundai to victory in the 2012 AFC Champions League. 
Kwak was one of the stand-out performers for Korea Republic in their run to the final 
of the AFC Asian Cup Australia 2015. Former Heerenveen, Manchester City and Celtic 
strike Georgios Samaras joined Al Hilal in February 2015 having represented Greece at 
the FIFA World Cup on two occasions.  
Coach: Giorgos Donis
Former Greece international Giorgos Donis took over the Al Hilal helm following the 
departure of Romanian Laurentiu Reghecampf ahead of the start of the AFC Champions 
League in February. Having coached in his homeland at Larissa, with whom he won 
the Greek Cup; AEK Athens, Atromitos and PAOK, Donis moved to Cypriot side APEOL 
where he claimed the league and cup double during the 2013/14 season and also was 
in charge of the side during their UEFA Champions League campaign.

Champions League once in five appearances and that was on their debut appearance in 
the revamped continental tournament in 2003. Kashima reached the quarter-finals in 
2008, losing to eventual finalists Adelaide United, before advancing to the Round of 
16 on their last three appearances in 2009, 2010 and 2011. Kashima qualified for the 
2015 edition having finished third in the J.League in 2014, although the Ibaraki club 
narrowly missed out on an eighth J.League title after finishing just three points behind 
champions Gamba Osaka after losing to Sagan Tosu on the final day.  
Key Players
Midfielder Gaku Shibasaki has been a fixture for Kashima Antlers in the last three 
seasons, but enjoyed a breakout year in 2014 and was named in the J.League’s Best XI. 
That earned Shibasaki a place in the Japan squad at the AFC Asian Cup Australia 2015 
where he made two appearances and scored in the quarter-final defeat by the United 
Arab Emirates. Veteran Takeshi Aoki is a one-club man who has been with Kashima since 
2001 and the defensive midfielder brings much-needed experience and leadership to a 
youthful side. Prior to the 2015 campaign, Aoki has already racked up nearly 30 AFC 
Champions League appearances for Kashima having played in 2008, 2009, 2010 and 
2011. Amongst Kashima’s biggest goal-threats will be Brazilian Caio after the forward 
was named the 2014 J.League Rookie of the Year and netted eight league goals.  
Coach: Toninho Cerezo 
Having coached Kashima Antlers to a pair of J.League titles including the Japanese 
treble in 2000, Toninho Cerezo returned for a second spell as coach of the Ibaraki club 
in 2013. And the former Brazil international midfielder, who enjoyed much success 
as a player with Italian sides Roma and Sampdoria, led Kashima to a third place finish 
in 2014. Cerezo has also coached in Saudi Arabia with Al Hilal and in the United Arab 
Emirates with Al Shabab, Al Arabi and Al Ain.

Early Years
Inaugurated in 1967, the Asian Club Championship was dominated by 
clubs from Israel with Hapoel Tel Aviv claiming the inaugural title after 
beating Malaysia’s Selangor in the final in Bangkok. Maccabi Tel Aviv then 
won twice in three years either side of Iran’s Taj – now known as Esteghlal 
- who beat Hapoel Tel Aviv. The competition then disappeared from the 
calendar after the 1971 edition.
Competition Reborn
The tournament returned in 1985 and Daewoo Royals of the newly formed 
Korean K-League – inaugurated in 1983 – became the first winners from 
East Asia after beating Saudi Arabia’s Al Ahli. The region dominated the 
competition in the late 1980’s with pre-J.League sides Furukawa and 
Yomiuri prevailing before Qatar’s Al Sadd broke the run in 1989.
West returns, Farmers rise
Clubs from Iran and Saudi Arabia came to the fore in the early 1990s as 
Tehran’s Esteghlal beat defending champions Liaoning of China in July 
1991, before losing their crown to Saudi Arabia’s Al Hilal. Pas of Iran beat 
Saudi side Al Shabab 1993, before Thai Farmers Bank claimed successive 
titles.
Professionalism brings success
The K-League dominated the mid-1990’s as Ilhwa Chunma lifted the 
trophy in 1995 before Pohang Steelers won back-to-back titles in 1997 
and 1998. Boosted by the foundation of professional leagues in Japan 
and China, the East won six of the last seven editions of the Asian Club 
Championship as Japan’s Jubilo Iwata reached three straight finals, while 
Saudi Arabia’s Al Hilal flew the flag for the West in 2000 after Jubilo’s 
1999 success. Korea Republic’s Suwon Samsung Bluewings won the last 
two editions of the Asian Club Championship in 2001 and 2001 after 
beating Jubilo and Anyang Cheetahs.
Champions League era
The Asian Club Championship was discontinued in 2002 and merged 
with the Asian Cup Winners’ Cup to form the AFC Champions League. 
Al Ain from the United Arab Emirates were victorious in the first final, 
which was delayed until October 2003 due to SARS and the Iraq War. The 
event followed a single year format from 2004, with Saudi Arabia’s Al 
Ittihad winning back-to-back titles in 2004 and 2005 before the East rose 
again as Korea’s Jeonbuk Hyundai Motors and Japanese duo Urawa Red 
Diamonds and Gamba Osaka lifted the prestigious trophy.
New format
In keeping with a rise in standards across the region, the AFC Champions 
League ushered in a new dynamic format in 2009 with only clubs and 
leagues professionally run as commercial entities eligible. Prize money 
was also increased and the competition expanded from 28 to 32 teams, 
while a one-leg Round of 16 and one-leg final were also introduced. The 
showpiece final was held in Tokyo in 2009 and 2010, with K-League clubs 
Pohang Steelers and then Seongnam Ilhwa Chunma victorious. 
Growth continues 
Jeonbuk earned the right to host the 2011 final, but missed out on 
keeping the trophy in Korean hands as Qatar’s Al Sadd became the first 
qualifiers to win the continental title. Korea were not to be denied a year 
later, though, claiming a third AFC Champions League crown in four years 
as Ulsan Hyundai completed an unbeaten campaign with a 3-0 win over 
Saudi Arabia’s Al Ahli. Korea were also represented in the 2013 final, but 
FC Seoul were edged out on away goals by Guangzhou Evergrande, who 
became the first Chinese side in 23 years to lift the title. The 2014 edition 
of the tournament was expanded with more teams eligible to participate 
in the play-off rounds, with Western Sydney Wanderers eventually 
becoming the first Australian side to crowned continental champions after 
beating Al Hilal 1-0 on aggregate.
